    Further reading

    The books, articles and community resources worth your time, roughly in the order you might read them.

    Books

    BookWhy read it
    Learning Domain-Driven Design, Vlad Khononov (O'Reilly, 2021)The most approachable modern introduction. It covers strategic and tactical design and connects DDD to microservices, event-driven architecture and data mesh. A good first book.
    Domain-Driven Design Distilled, Vaughn Vernon (Addison-Wesley, 2016)Short and practical. Read it in an afternoon to get the whole picture.
    Domain-Driven Design: Tackling Complexity in the Heart of Software, Eric Evans (Addison-Wesley, 2003)The original "blue book". Dense, but the source of every idea here. Parts III and IV (supple design, strategic design) are often underrated.
    Implementing Domain-Driven Design, Vaughn Vernon (Addison-Wesley, 2013)The "red book". Detailed implementation guidance, including the aggregate design rules used in this guide.
    Domain Modeling Made Functional, Scott Wlaschin (Pragmatic Bookshelf, 2018)DDD with types in F#. Excellent on making illegal states unrepresentable, and useful in any language.
    Architecture Patterns with Python, Harry Percival & Bob Gregory (O'Reilly, 2020)Repository, unit of work, events and CQRS built step by step with tests. Free online at cosmicpython.com.
    Introducing EventStorming, Alberto Brandolini (Leanpub)From the creator of Event Storming.
    Domain Storytelling, Stefan Hofer & Henning Schwentner (Addison-Wesley, 2021)A complete guide to the technique.
    Team Topologies, Matthew Skelton & Manuel Pais (IT Revolution, 2019)Team design that complements context mapping and Conway's law.

    Articles & essays

    • Effective Aggregate Design (parts I–III), Vaughn Vernon: the origin of the aggregate rules.
    • Getting Started with DDD When Surrounded by Legacy Systems, Eric Evans: bubble and autonomous bubble contexts.
    • Anemic Domain Model, Bounded Context, CQRS, Strangler Fig Application: short essays on martinfowler.com.
    • Hexagonal Architecture, Alistair Cockburn: the original ports & adapters article.
    • Domain-Driven Design Reference, Eric Evans: a free summary of all the patterns, written by Evans in 2015.

    Community resources

    • DDD Crew (github.com/ddd-crew): free, open templates and guides, including the Bounded Context Canvas, Aggregate Design Canvas, Context Mapping cheat sheet, Core Domain Charts and the DDD Starter Modelling Process.
    • Virtual DDD, DDD Europe and Explore DDD: meetups and conference talks, many recorded and free online.
    • Context Mapper (contextmapper.org): a DSL and tooling for context maps.

    How to practise

    1. Pick a domain you know well from outside work: a sports league, a library, your favourite shop.
    2. Run a solo Event Storming on paper. Find the hotspots.
    3. Draw the subdomains and a context map. Decide which context is core.
    4. Model that one context's aggregates with the Aggregate Design Canvas.
    5. Implement it with tests that read like rules. Then change a requirement and see how the model copes.
